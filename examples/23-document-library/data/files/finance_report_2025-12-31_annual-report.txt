Annual report 2025

The year in brief. Revenue grew by eleven per cent, most of it from the two markets opened in 2024. Operating costs grew by six per cent, which is below the plan the board agreed in January, and headcount rose from 212 to 240 people across four offices.

Customers. The number of paying customers passed nine thousand in October. Churn fell for the third year running, and the support team answered ninety-four per cent of requests within one working day. The customer survey was sent to every account and returned by a third of them.

People. Two hundred and forty people work for the company at the end of the year. Twenty-eight of them joined through the graduate programme, and the engineering team moved to a four-day on-site week in the spring after a trial in the Lisbon office.

Products. Three releases shipped this year. The largest of them replaced the reporting module, which had been in service since 2019, and moved every customer onto the new billing system without a day of downtime.

Outlook. The plan for 2026 assumes growth of nine per cent and a second office in Asia, and it keeps the cost of hiring within the budget the board has already approved.

Risks and treasury. The audit committee approved a reserve fund equal to four months of operating costs, held in cash, and asked for the currency exposure of the new markets to be reported every quarter.
