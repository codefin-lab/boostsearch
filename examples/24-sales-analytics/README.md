# 24. A sales report built from aggregations alone

A year of order lines -- 3,004 of them, one document per line sold -- and the
report a finance team asks for at the end of it: revenue by region, category
and month, which pairs of region and channel carry the business, what hardly
sold, where the margin is and where it is being given away in discounts. No
row leaves the engine; every number is an aggregation, and the two summaries
are kept by jobs the engine runs itself -- a transform and a rollup.

The data is generated by `data/make-sales.py` with a fixed seed, and the
output is committed beside it, so every number below can be recounted by
anyone from the file itself. `run.sh` does not just print the answers: each
step states what it expects, and those expectations were counted from the data
by a separate script, not copied from a run.

Example 02 covers pipeline aggregations over time and `significant_terms`;
example 03 covers facets over `nested` documents. This one covers the rest.

## What it shows

| Step | Feature |
|---|---|
| 1 | a `strict` mapping where every field is a `keyword`, a number or a `date` |
| 2 | `_bulk` from a committed, regenerable data file |
| 3 | `composite` with `terms` and `date_histogram` sources, paged with `after` / `after_key` to the end |
| 4 | `_plugins/_transform`: a summary index kept by a job, one document per bucket, its run explained |
| 5 | the same request against the raw index and the summary, with the same answers; `bucket_sort` over a `date_histogram` |
| 6 | `_plugins/_rollup`: a rollup of every day and region, searched as though it were the raw index; its refusal of `size` |
| 7 | `multi_terms` ordered by a sub-aggregation |
| 8 | `rare_terms` with `max_doc_count` |
| 9 | `auto_date_histogram` choosing its own interval |
| 10 | `range` and `date_range` with `keyed: true` and named keys |
| 11 | `top_hits` per bucket, sorted two different ways |
| 12 | `weighted_avg`, `median_absolute_deviation`, `percentiles`, `extended_stats` with `sigma`, `matrix_stats` |
| 13 | `bucket_script`, `bucket_selector` and `bucket_sort` ranking regions by margin |
| 14 | `filters` with `other_bucket_key` |
| 15 | `global` against the query's scope |
| 16 | `missing` on `terms` and `avg`, and the `missing` aggregation |
| 17 | `terms` over a Painless `script` -- a value no document stores |

## Running it

```bash
make serve      # a node configured for this example, port 9284, foreground
make run        # the example, in another terminal
```

`make check` validates the script and every request body without a server,
and confirms the committed data is byte for byte what the generator writes;
`make clean` deletes what the example left behind. Copy `.env.example` to
`.env` to change the address.

The longer form. The example needs nothing special of the node:

```bash
BOOSTSEARCH_ADDR=127.0.0.1:9284 ./target/release/boostsearch &
BS=http://127.0.0.1:9284 examples/24-sales-analytics/run.sh
```

## What to look for

- **Step 3** exports all 240 region x category x month buckets in pages of
  100: 100, 100, 40, then an empty page that ends the loop. The buckets add up
  to 3,004 sales and 1,030,908.54 of revenue, exactly what the raw index holds,
  and no key appears on two pages. A `terms` aggregation would have had to
  guess a `size` big enough and send everything in one answer.
- **Step 4** is a transform: `requests/16-a-transform-that-keeps-the-summary.json`
  names the groups (region, category, and `sold_at` by calendar month) and what
  to compute in each, and the node runs it on its schedule and turns it off
  once it has run. Its `_explain` says what it did: 3,004 sales read, 240
  documents written, in 4 pages -- three pages of 100 buckets and the empty
  page that says there are no more. Each document's id is worked out from its
  key, so a second run overwrites the first rather than doubling it.
- **Step 5** asks the same question of `sales` and of `sales-monthly` and gets
  the same five regional totals (north 317,971.62, central 111,189.49, ...)
  from 240 documents instead of 3,004. That is the whole case for a summary
  index. The best three months come from the summary alone: December
  (148,871.00), November, October.
- **Step 6** is a rollup of every day and region: 1,382 documents, one per
  day a region sold something. Searching `sales-daily-rollup` with the request
  step 5 sent to the raw index gives the same regional revenue *and the same
  counts* -- 932 sales in the north -- because each rollup document carries how
  many sales it stands for. An `avg` is put back together from the sums and
  counts the rollup kept (341.17 per sale in the north). What a rollup cannot
  answer it refuses: asked for `size: 10`, it says a rollup search must have
  size 0, because it has no single sales to show.
- **Step 8** names `globe` and `telescope` (one sale each) and `hammock` (two)
  and nothing else. `terms` ordered by ascending count would find them too on
  one shard, but is not reliable across shards; `rare_terms` is built for it.
- **Step 9** asks for at most six buckets over a year, and the engine answers
  with four quarters (`"interval": "3M"`): 585, 636, 678 and 1,105
  sales. Monthly would have been twelve. Step 17 computes the same quarters in a script and
  gets the same counts, which is a good way to check a script.
- **Step 12** is the difference between a plain average and a weighted one.
  Electronics sells at an average unit price of 273.67 per line, but 254.52 per
  unit sold: the large orders happened to be for cheaper lines, and only the
  weighted average sees it. Discounts average 0.082 with a standard deviation of
  0.0575 and a maximum of 0.31.
- **Step 13** is the regional ranking. By revenue north leads; by margin
  percentage west does (36.17%), then north (33.98%) and east (32.59%).
  `bucket_selector` has already dropped south (28.84%) and central (24.12%)
  before `bucket_sort` ranks what is left.
- **Step 14** answers 244 bulk orders, 127 deeply discounted ones and 2,642
  that are neither. That is 3,013 for 3,004 sales: `filters` buckets overlap,
  and nine sales are both bulk and deeply discounted. `other_bucket` is only
  the documents that matched no filter.
- **Step 15** is two scopes in one request. The query restricts the first
  numbers to central (111,189.49 of revenue, discount 0.178); `global` ignores
  the query and gives the company (1,030,908.54, discount 0.082). Central
  discounts more than twice as deeply, which is why step 13 dropped it.
- **Step 16**: 574 sales were never rated and 2,553 used no coupon. The same
  field averages 3.96 over the sales that were rated and 3.40 if silence counts
  as a 1. Neither is wrong; they answer different questions, and `missing`
  is where you say which one you are asking.
- **The median and its deviation are estimates.** Step 12 prints 72.87 and
  50.74; counted exactly they are 72.82 and 50.66. OpenSearch gives 72.45 and
  50.55 from the same data, because both engines summarise the values in a
  sketch rather than sorting them. Do not reconcile a report against either
  to the cent.

## What this node does not do yet

These were tried on the same data and left out of `run.sh`. OpenSearch 3.8
answers all of them.

| Asked for | This node answers |
|---|---|
| `top_metrics` | `400`, unknown aggregation type; step 11 uses `top_hits` |
| `top_hits` under `rare_terms` or `composite` | `400` asking for `sort`; given one, `400` refusing `_source`; given neither, `200` with `hits` as `[{"sort":[null]}]`. Under `terms` and `multi_terms` it works |
| `variable_width_histogram` | buckets that differ from OpenSearch's on the same data |
| `missing` on a `histogram` | ignored: the unrated sales do not appear in bucket 0; step 15 uses `terms` |
| `format` on `min` / `max` over a date | ignored: `value_as_string` is the full timestamp |

## Leaves behind

The indices `sales` (3,004 documents), `sales-monthly` (240) and
`sales-daily-rollup` (1,382), and the transform `sales-monthly` and the rollup
job `sales-daily`, both finished and turned off. Rerunning stops and deletes the
jobs and the indices first. `/tmp/sales-24-buckets.ndjson` holds the exported
composite buckets; it is rewritten on every run.
